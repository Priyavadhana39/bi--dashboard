# Supplier Evaluation Guidelines

## Purpose

This document provides practical guidelines for evaluating and managing supplier relationships based on data-driven insights from the Procurement Intelligence System.

## Understanding Supplier Scores

### Score Interpretation

The ML-generated supplier score (0-100) combines multiple factors:

- **80-100 (Excellent)**: Top-tier suppliers, prioritize for critical orders
- **50-80 (Good)**: Reliable suppliers, suitable for most categories
- **25-50 (Average)**: Acceptable but monitor closely
- **0-25 (Poor)**: High risk, consider alternatives

### Score Components

Each score reflects:
- 30% Cost savings performance
- 25% Quality metrics (defect rates)
- 20% Delivery performance (lead times)
- 15% Compliance with regulations
- 10% Data completeness

---

## When to Replace a Supplier

### Red Flags

Consider replacing a supplier if:

1. **Performance Score Below 25**: Consistent poor performance
2. **Multiple Price Anomalies**: Pattern of unusual pricing
3. **High Defect Rate**: >15% defect rate over 6 months
4. **Compliance Issues**: <70% compliance rate
5. **Delivery Problems**: >50% late deliveries

### Replacement Process

1. Document performance issues with data
2. Discuss concerns with current supplier
3. Issue formal improvement notice (30-day review period)
4. Identify alternative suppliers with better scores
5. Pilot orders with new supplier
6. Full transition if pilot successful

---

## Negotiation Strategies

### Data-Driven Negotiation

Use system data to negotiate:

- **Price Benchmarking**: Show category average prices
- **Volume Leverage**: Reference total order volume
- **Quality Comparison**: Compare defect rates with competitors
- **Performance History**: Highlight on-time delivery record

### When You Have Leverage

Stronger negotiating position when:
- Supplier has poor ML performance score
- Multiple alternative suppliers available with higher scores
- Price anomalies suggest overpricing
- High data completeness gives clear performance picture

## Category-Specific Considerations

### Electronics
- Prioritize quality over cost savings
- Verify technical specifications compliance
- Require supplier quality certifications
- Minimum supplier score: 60

### Raw Materials
- Focus on supply chain reliability
- Monitor price volatility using anomaly detection
- Require inventory safety stock agreements
- Minimum supplier score: 50

### Office Supplies / MRO
- Emphasize cost savings and lead time
- Allow more flexibility on quality metrics
- Consider supplier consolidation opportunities
- Minimum supplier score: 40

### Packaging
- Balance cost and quality
- Ensure environmental compliance
- Review lead times for production planning
- Minimum supplier score: 50

---

## Best Practices

### Regular Monitoring

- **Weekly**: Review new price anomalies
- **Monthly**: Check supplier score changes
- **Quarterly**: Full supplier performance review
- **Annually**: Strategic supplier relationship assessment

### Documentation

Always document:
- Reasons for supplier selection
- Price negotiation outcomes
- Quality issues and resolutions
- Delivery problems and improvements
- Communication with suppliers

### Proactive Management

- Address performance issues early
- Use demand forecasts for order planning
- Share performance data with suppliers
- Reward excellent performance with increased orders

## Using the AI Procurement Agent

### Effective Questions

Ask the AI agent:
- "Why is [Supplier X] performing poorly?"
- "Which supplier should we use for [Category]?"
- "Are there price anomalies for [Supplier/Category]?"
- "What is the demand forecast for [Category]?"

### Interpreting Agent Responses

The agent provides:
- **Evidence**: Historical data and facts
- **ML Insights**: Scores, anomalies, forecasts
- **Recommendations**: Data-driven suggestions
- **Limitations**: Uncertainties and missing data

Always review the evidence and use professional judgment alongside agent recommendations.
